Our research · July 2026

We didn't guess. We asked.

Before building anything, our volunteer team surveyed 103 migrants and foreign residents in Basel and the Basel-Stadt / Basel-Landschaft region — on the street, at workplaces, at the university, and online. The goal: understand their real needs and challenges.

01 · Method

Street-level, face to face

Most answers were collected in person: street interviews with passers-by, labour migrants and students, complemented by online platforms and direct community outreach. The survey ran in July 2026 at these locations:

  • Barfüsserplatz
  • Rheinufer / Kleinbasel (around Claraplatz)
  • Roche headquarters, Grenzacherstrasse 124
  • Faculty of Business and Economics, University of Basel
  • kHaus, Kasernenstrasse 8

103 respondents is a snapshot, not a census. But the pattern was consistent across locations, ages and permit types.

03 · What the study shows

Four findings that shaped BOOST

79%

The support gap

79% did not contact an integration organisation in the past 12 months. And when respondents rated their experiences with integration organisations on a 1–5 scale, the average score was 3.4. Support exists — but it isn't reaching people, and when it does, it underwhelms.

~75%

The loneliness that stays

Around 75% of participants in their second year felt lonely at least sometimes; roughly 40% experience loneliness about once a month. Two years in, the paperwork may be done — the sense of belonging often isn't.

6+ yrs

The barriers that don't expire

Language, taxes and bureaucracy remain difficult even after 6+ years in Switzerland. "Integration" isn't a phase that ends after the welcome period — the system stays complex for years.

56%

The informal lifeline

56% rely on personal networks. Most people discover support through friends, social media and the internet rather than official channels. Trust lives in peer networks — which is exactly where help should meet people.

What this means

The gap isn't information. It's connection.

People aren't helpless — they're resourceful. They solve Swiss life through friends and group chats. What's missing is a bridge: informal, trustworthy, human access to the knowledge and networks official channels can't deliver alone.

That's why our first programme is Admin Night: peer support and pro-bono expertise, in a room that feels like the way people actually help each other.
